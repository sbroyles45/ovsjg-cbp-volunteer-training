Volunteers: Your Most Powerful Untapped Resources

A two-part training series for DC nonprofits on finding volunteers, and on building the policies and program that keep them. Everything from both sessions, recordings, worksheets, prompts, and tools, lives here.

Each recording has closed captions, with a settings gear to translate them into dozens of languages. For a full transcript, open the recording on YouTube and choose "Show transcript" from the "..." menu below it.

Download the fillable worksheets before filling them in; Google Drive's preview will not accept typed answers. Open the file in Preview, Acrobat Reader, or your browser's PDF viewer; no paid software needed.

Resource Guides

The organizations, platforms, and contacts named in each session, collected in one place with working links.

  • DC Nonprofit Volunteer Resource Guide, Part One: Finding Them Where to recruit general volunteers (Serve DC's portal, AmeriCorps, Idealist, JustServe); the DC-area university and graduate-program pipeline for interns in social work, public health, law, and communications; pro bono legal resources (the DC Bar Pro Bono Center, area law school clinics) and pro bono consulting (Compass Pro Bono and others); board and leadership contacts; national days of service to build a recruitment push around; and how to make an opening visible, from neighborhood platforms to earned media. PDF
  • DC Nonprofit Volunteer Resource Guide, Part Two: Building the Program Sorting your volunteer needs into direct service, operations, and advocacy; writing a role description with the six elements every posting and screening decision depends on; scaling screening and onboarding to the role's risk level, with foundation policies and a volunteer agreement; posting the role, and where; a separate look at interns, stipends, and Federal Work-Study, what's confirmed and what to take to counsel; tracking and valuing volunteer hours with the free Hour Tracking Log template and the verified $54.77/hour DC rate; and sustaining the program through retention, a leadership pipeline, and documentation. PDF
Session 1 · August 18

Expand Your Team Without Expanding Your Budget

Tuesday, August 18, 2026, 11:00 am · Panel with Serve DC

Where to find volunteers, interns, and pro bono support in Washington DC. Stephen Broyles of The Magi Group walked through the DC volunteer and intern platforms, then George Garcia and Frances Whalen from Serve DC, the Mayor's Office on Volunteerism and Partnerships, joined to cover Serve DC's Galaxy Digital portal, which connects more than 300 DC organizations with a roster of over 12,000 volunteers. The session also covered the DC-area university internship pipeline, pro bono legal and consulting resources including the DC Bar Pro Bono Center and Compass Pro Bono, and how to make an organization visible to volunteers and respond well when one reaches out.

Session 2 · August 25

From Chaos to Coordinated: Building Your Volunteer Program

Tuesday, August 25, 2026, 11:00 am to 12:30 pm

The nuts and bolts of a volunteer program: writing role descriptions, matching screening to risk, the policies a program needs in writing, tracking and recognizing hours, and what actually drives retention and growth. Stephen Broyles of The Magi Group walked through a six-step volunteer framework: define your needs, write the role, screen and onboard, post the opportunity, track the hours, and sustain and grow the program.
